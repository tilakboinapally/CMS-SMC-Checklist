What counts as an HIE module (SMC Guidance, Table 1)

“Electronic exchange of clinical information that allows health care providers and patients to access and securely share a patient's medical information, provided the Medicaid agency owns, operates, and actively uses the HIE. If the Medicaid agency only receives reports from HIE, it is classified as a service.”

The 7 capability descriptions — capabilities, NOT requirements

The HIE module page describes seven capabilities. They are capability descriptions, not CMS-required outcomes — no state is certified against this list.

CapabilityWhat CMS describes
1 · Event Notification ServicesADT (admit / discharge / transfer) alerts for empaneled patients.
2 · Care Coordination / Clinical Data ExchangePortal / in-workflow viewer; CCDs are the most frequent exchange, including advance-directive / MOLST registries.
3 · Direct Secure MessagingMessaging over the Direct protocol.
4 · Public Health — Registries and ReportingImmunizations, electronic lab reporting (eLR), syndromic surveillance, electronic case reporting (eCR), and PDMP connections.
5 · Population Health Analytics / Support to Value-Based CareAnalytics that support value-based care arrangements.
6 · Quality ReportingStandardized clinical quality metrics.
7 · Emergency ResponseFor example, PULSE (Patient Unified Lookup System for Emergencies) for displaced patients.

The module page also notes closed-loop referrals for health-related social needs, and inpatient / sub-acute bed-availability registries.

What an Asset Verification System is

States must establish and use an Asset Verification System (AVS) to verify assets held in a financial institution for individuals whose eligibility is based on being age 65 or over, being blind, or having a disability, and who are subject to an asset test. Legal basis: sections 1137, 1940, and 1902(a)(46)(A) of the Social Security Act; implementing regulations at 42 C.F.R. §§ 435.940 through 435.952 and 457.380. Source: CMS CMCS Informational Bulletin, November 20, 2024, “Financial Eligibility Verification Requirements and Flexibilities” — medicaid.gov (PDF) ↗

How it works in practice: the system automatically pulls bank-account balances and other financial-institution data for aged/blind/disabled applicants — looking back 60 months/5 years in the District of Columbia’s plan — and compares the total to the resource limit ($4,000 individual / $6,000 married couple). If the AVS result is reasonably compatible with what the applicant attested, no further proof is needed; if it is above the limit and not reasonably compatible, the agency must give the applicant notice and a chance to document or dispute the findings before denying or ending coverage (42 CFR 435.952(d)).

AVS is a function inside Eligibility & Enrollment, not a standalone module. It is one of the electronic data sources in EE5 (Electronic Verification) — the eligibility system’s automated interfaces include “data from financial institutions for asset verification” — and EE5 also notes that Puerto Rico must implement an Asset Verification System by 2026 under the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2023, Section 5101.

About these lists

Important: the old checklist is retired

The Medicaid Enterprise Certification Toolkit (MECT) — including its 2007 Chapter 5 “Provider Management Checklist” — was sunset by CMS on 14 April 2022 (SMD #22-001). How proof works — the short version

  • CMS does not prescribe one proof method per item. The normal package is a combination: a live system demo (pre-production at the ORR, in production at the Certification Review) + metric values in the Operational Report Workbook (ORW) + a named evidence file in the CMS Box repository, cited on the SMC Intake Form.
  • CEFs are different: CMS says CEF criteria do not require demonstrations — the filed document, attestation, or third-party assessment is enough.
  • Only two PM metrics name a non-numeric artifact: PM9 (screenshots of high-risk providers) and PM16 (copies of notices). The only mandatory third-party proof is security/privacy — CEF09 / CEF12.
  • “N/A” is a formal adjudication, not an omission — the state marks it on the Intake Form with a written justification, for both reviews (see the reviews section below).

The full six-method taxonomy is on this page below. The ORR/CR mechanics are in The two reviews in the left menu, and state examples are in State examples — labelled as vendor claims or procurement documents. CMS itself publishes no state-specific PM outcomes and no state metric values (verified 24 Nov 2026).

  • Self-attestation (alone or as stated alternative) — 5 CEFs: CEF02, CEF03, CEF08, CEF11, CEF22.

  • Refer-to-outcomes / N/A-prone — 4 CEFs: CEF04, CEF14, CEF16, CEF17 (CEF17 is always N/A for a PM module).

  • Document / sample artifact in CMS Box — where the metric says “provide a copy / copies / sample”: EPS3 (managed-care contract clauses), EPS5 (filed federal reports), MM2, MM5, MM9 (sample notices), MM10 (directory extract), PDMP3 (interstate data-sharing agreements), PDMP4 (access-issues report), TPL12 (agreements + periodic risk analysis), PI9 (RAC report), and the borrowed FM5 (repayment aging report) and PBM9 (sample report) in the PI list.
  • EVV evidence — test reports, screenshots, explanations, training records: EVV01–EVV09 are proved with automated test reports, screenshots, written explanations, and training materials, with a demonstration in the production environment at the Certification Review. The measures are the four named metrics on the EVV module page — not the placeholder Metric IDs in the EVV Intake Form (see the EVV section).
  • HIE — no items to group: CMS publishes no CMS-required outcomes for HIE, so there is nothing to prove against a CMS list; states define their own outcomes and metrics (see the HIE section).

Three rules that govern every method

  • Evidence ≠ Required Artifacts. Evidence demonstrates an outcome or condition; Required Artifacts (Certification Request Letter, System Acceptance Letter, Monthly Project Status Reports, Master Test Plan, Test Summary Reports, Deployment Plan, Defect/Risk/Issue Lists) describe the project’s progression and are not typically used as evidence.
  • Cite the exact Box filename (not a hyperlink) in the Intake Form evidence column, pointing to the relevant section of long documents. A state may reuse existing DDI/RFP deliverables as evidence — no separate “wraparound” file is needed. CEF files must not be redacted, including screenshots/reports containing PHI/PII.
  • Not every outcome needs a metric (FAQ Q20): one metric may serve multiple outcomes, and a state may propose alternative metrics to its State Officer — but must do so before reporting them in the OAPD. Any change to outcomes/metrics requires an APD-Update.

The two reviews — ORR vs. Certification Review (all modules)

The same Intake Form is adjudicated twice for every module: once before go-live, once after at least six months in production.

Who reviews the evidence

  • CMS MES State Officer — the state’s standing CMS contact: APD approval, outcome/metric finalisation, monthly project-status reports, entry-criteria confirmation, T-MSIS liaison, post-certification reporting.
  • CMS Certification Team — runs the ORR and CR, sends the IRL, questions the demos, and records its evaluation/adjudication on the Intake Form.
  • CMS T-MSIS team (via the State Officer) — confirms OBA compliance for CR entry.
  • Independent third-party security/privacy assessor — not a CMS reviewer, but its SAR/pen-test/POA&M is required evidence; the assessor organisation cannot be the one performing DDI.
  • No IV&V is required by SMC itself — states often procure it separately (see Missouri in the state examples).

Adjudication & “N/A with justification”

  • The Intake Form is the official record: the state fills the State ORR/CR Evidence and Comments columns; the Certification Team records its adjudication in the CMS columns.
  • The only adjudication labels CMS publishes verbatim are the EVV ones: “Evidence Satisfactory,” “Evidence Not Satisfactory,” and “Not Applicable.” CMS has not published a separate general-SMC label list. “Not Satisfactory” does not automatically mean denial — CMS follows up on remediations before assembling the certification package.
  • N/A rule: mark “N/A” in the State ORR and CR Evidence columns and justify it in the Comments column for both reviews — CMS must accept the justification. CMS’s own worked example: CEF17 is an Eligibility & Enrollment condition and does not apply to a Provider Management module.
  • A CEF met at ORR need not be re-demonstrated at CR unless there are significant changes, a long time-lapse, a new annual DR test, or a new biennial security assessment.

How the lists fit together

  • Module outcomes — module-specific. Based on statute/regulation; they form the baseline a system must meet to be certified and keep enhanced funding for operations. This page covers Provider Management (21), Claims Processing (6), Pharmacy Benefit Management (9), Eligibility and Enrollment (27), Financial Management (9), Decision Support / Data Warehouse (2), Encounter Processing (5), Long-term Services and Supports (11), Member Management (11), Prescription Drug Monitoring Program (6), Third-party Liability (13), Program Integrity (18 rows), Health Information Exchange (0 CMS-required outcomes), and Electronic Visit Verification (9).
  • CEF — apply to every MES module, under 42 CFR §433.112 (build) and §433.116 (operations). One shared list of 22 — never duplicated per module. Non-applicable CEFs are marked “N/A” with justification.
  • Process: APD approval → state populates the SMC Intake Form with all outcomes + CEFs → Operational Readiness Review (ORR) → production → Certification Review (CR). The finalized form is due at least 2 weeks before the review and becomes the official record.
